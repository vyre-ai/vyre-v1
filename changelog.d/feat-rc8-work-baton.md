Work now passes a baton in a task thread, and agents wake when it reaches them. Every task has its own thread, the one place for its brief, hand-overs and fixes; it stays out of Recent chats, and the people and agents on the task (Assigned, Reviewer, Approver) are in it. Agents get five tools to end their part: task_submit hands in the outputs with a one-line summary (checked against the schema the task asks for), task_ask puts a question to the requester or a named person and the work waits for the answer, task_block reports what is in the way, task_review approves or sends work back with numbered fixes (after the review loop limit a person decides), and task_decide approves or rejects with a reason. When the baton lands on an agent, it is woken in the task thread and told its role, what the task is and which tool to finish with; a group or role is offered to its agent members and the first to take the task holds it; a finished question wakes the work that waited on it. Each hand-over is also written as a line in the thread. An agent that ends its turn without handing the task on is nudged once in the thread and woken again; if the task still has not moved after the step's nudge time (30 minutes by default), a person gets a task in Needs you, the one the step names or the person who asked. A Run waiting on a task now goes on when the task is Done or Canceled by any route, not only when it is approved or answered. The Create a task step can wait for its task and go on with what the doer handed in (its outputs and summary). The AI step now builds its brief from the step and the Run instead of joining text: Goal, Context (the Automation, what started the Run, linked records and files, earlier steps), Inputs, Deliverable (the output fields as a list with a small example), Done means, Next, Rules and, for an agent task, how to finish. Secret-looking values in the data are hidden and no key or account is ever read into it. An AI step can now be an agent task instead of a quick answer: it makes a task for the step's agent with the brief, lets the agent work in the task thread with its tools, waits until the task is Done (through its Reviewer and Approver, if the step names them) and returns what was submitted. The step's nudge time and escalation person are used when the agent stalls. The quick answer stays as it was. On the task page, a work task shows a stepper (Do, Review, Approve, Done) with the face of whoever holds each step and the current holder lit, the brief collapsed under the title, what was handed in, the fixes as a checklist, and the buttons for the person whose turn it is: hand in, approve or send back with fixes, approve or reject with a reason. A Run shows who holds the baton on its row and lists the tasks it made, and a task opens beside the run. The AI step card has an Answer or Task switch; a task has a goal with an Insert data picker, seats for who does, reviews and approves (with faces), the deliverable as a list of fields, what done means, and time limit, nudge and escalation under More. Fix round: the AI step card names the people and agents on a task instead of showing ids, the Goal shows inserted data as chips, the step's summary line is one short line, and the deliverable's field types are picked from a menu (text, number, whole number, yes/no, date, list, record link) with the raw shape under Advanced. The task page keeps notes to one Add a note line and shows faces on the seats. My work lists the tasks whose baton is with you first, and an agent's page shows what it is working on.
