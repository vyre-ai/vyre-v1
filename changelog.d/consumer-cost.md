- A record event reads its type once for the projects consumer, the search consumer asks the schema for a title field once in ten seconds, and the rows of one indexed record are one transaction: the consumers cost about 20 % less a bulk-written row.
- Measured on the mini-shaped bulk write (1,500 contacts, thread CPU): the inline reducers cost 0.23 ms a row (field meta 0.094, rows 0.075, sync changes 0.05), WAL sync and checkpoint syscalls about a quarter of the write CPU (2.55 s to 1.93 s with synchronous off, which is not used), the stand-in embedder 30 to 50 microseconds a document, and committing 25 rows together was not done (the consumers write while a group is open).
- A type whose title field changes has its indexed records titled again, a page of a hundred every three seconds (search.titles marks it, search.retitle does the pages), so they are found by the new title without being edited.
- The first start after this update queues every record type that has indexed documents once for the paged re-titling, so a title field that changed before the update is put right too, and the field the documents were titled by is recorded for all.
- The rows of one indexed document (the failure mark, the sequence, the old entry out, the new one and its text in) are one transaction: a file made by `files.create_many` is indexed in one commit, not five or six.
