Bring a CRM into Records. An agent fills one document, a sync spec, that says which objects the app has, where the list and the ids sit in its answers, how it pages (page numbers, a cursor, an offset or a Link header), which fields map to which record fields, which choices and pipeline stages exist, and which direction each field syncs. The spec holds the Vault handle, never the key. Vyre checks the spec against a real page of the app with read-only requests (the list, the ids, the second page, every mapped field and its type), then compiles it into a connector with its own tests, a connection, record types with fields, choices, relations and stages, and the synced-type bindings, and starts the first pull. Nothing is written to the app. Writes are compiled only where the spec cites the docs that show them, deletes stay off unless turned on per object, and a relation is read-only. The same spec applied again keeps the connector version; a changed one adds a version and only adds the fields a type lacks. 

To fill the spec from real pages, the agent has two read-only discovery helpers that use the Vault handle and can only send GET: sync_specs.sample reads one page (where the lists are, what an item holds and the types of its fields, how the next page is asked for, a total, and field-metadata or schema endpoints) and sync_specs.survey looks at up to 12 paths at once.

The sync pass now actually runs. Starting a sync schedules it in the background as the person who asked and finishes its Run with a one-line result or the reason it failed; a failure also shows in the sync log. A synced type keeps itself current by polling (one poll ahead at a time, sooner while pushes wait, ended by a pause, started again by a resume). A relation to another synced type becomes a real link to the record pulled for it, fills in as soon as that record arrives, and moves at both ends (the organization lists its people). A pass reads the app's list once, waits and tries again when the app or the connection says slow down, and stops when an app answers the same page again. When both sides changed a field and the newest change wins, the value that lost stays in the field's history. A record the record type refuses is skipped and logged instead of stopping the pass, and an app that ignores the page parameter no longer loops. New capabilities: connectors.sync_run and connectors.sync_poll (called by the scheduler).

What a person sees of a synced type is now answerable: sync_types.record_state says where one record came from (with the address to open it in the app when the spec gave one) and how each field is doing (saved, saving, failed, only here); sync_types.field_history lists the earlier values a newest-wins settlement replaced and sync_types.restore_field puts one back and sends it; sync_types.status is the Sync page (last pass, counts, failures, pushes waiting, whether the first-pull filter still holds, the next poll); sync_specs.remove stops syncing and keeps the records as plain records or removes them with their types.

New capabilities: sync_specs.validate, sync_specs.preview, sync_specs.apply, sync_specs.get, sync_specs.backfill, sync_specs.sample, sync_specs.survey, sync_specs.remove, sync_types.record_state, sync_types.field_history, sync_types.restore_field, sync_types.status (generated files need regenerating).

A new built-in skill, crm-sync-builder, runs the whole thing for the person. It checks what exists, reads the app's docs, discovers objects and fields from real pages (including field-metadata, pipelines and custom objects), proposes in one short message with tappable replies (Looks good, Change direction, Add the objects left out, Show me fields), shows fields only when asked, previews with three sample records and a read-only proof, applies, brings the first pull in the background and keeps the type live. Three ways in: Sync to Records next to Add to Automations on a Vault key, Bring in from your CRM in the Records sidebar (for people who can change record types), and asking in any chat.

In Records, a type that syncs from an app wears the app's logo with a quiet sync dot (green in step, blue while it works, amber when something needs you, grey when paused); pressing the dot opens the type's Sync page. A record shows a source chip with the app and Open in the app. Beside each synced field a small mark says where it stands: a lock when the app owns it (read-only), a spinner while it is on its way, a check once saved ("Saved to Ledger 2 s ago" on hover), a red mark that retries when it could not be sent; a field whose value a newest-wins settlement replaced shows a dot, and pressing it lists the earlier values with Restore. The Sync page shows the app, how many records have come in of how many it has, a progress bar, the first-pull filter with Bring in everything, the last pass and the next check, what waits and what failed (with Retry and Cancel), what was just sent (with Undo), and Pause or Resume, Resync, Edit mapping (the assistant opens with the spec) and Remove (keep the records as plain records, or remove them with a copy in Trash for 30 days).

A big app is pulled a group of pages at a time, not as one list in memory: the first pull and a backfill read about 2,000 records per group, keep their place so a restart carries on, and report "Catching up" on the Sync page until the last group lands. Records the app sorts first (newest, when the spec sorts that way) arrive first. A group never marks a record removed and never pushes.

A spec may say who the new record types are open to (`plan.audience`: the whole Space, only people added, or only the person applying); apply sets it through the Records sharing capability. In a Team Space the assistant asks first.
