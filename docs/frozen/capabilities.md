# Frozen capabilities (Freeze review A, P-PR_34)

Review of the merged capability registry at the F11 freeze. `packages/contracts/tests/frozen-capabilities.test.ts` checks the rules in this file and the snapshots it describes. A deliberate change to the registry updates this file in the same commit, signed by the lead.

## Frozen values

The contract of every capability is frozen in `docs/frozen/snapshots/<manifest>.json`: one small file per manifest, one line per capability (`name@version`) holding the sha256 of its canonical definition with shared schemas resolved (`lib/snapshot.ts`). The regen bot writes the files with `node tools/gen/src/cli.ts --snapshots`; the test fails on a missing, changed or stray file and names the capability. There is no registry-wide hash, count or per-lane table here on purpose: each of those changed with every contract change, so every contract PR conflicted with every other. Two PRs that change different manifests now merge in either order with nothing to re-record.

## Completeness (all capabilities, checked by the test)

- Every capability has an input schema, an output schema, at least one example, at least one declared error, an effects value (read, write or outward) and a level.
- Permission is a catalogue key or null (grant-only); both are valid, null means the grant level alone decides.
- Every outward capability has a gate; 47 of them use `gate.act = none` and each of those carries a written reason.
- Every capability with idempotency `key` has an `idempotencyKey` in its input (through `$ref` where the schema is shared).
- Every capability has exactly one example, so no capability is unexemplified.

## Sample read at the freeze

At the F11 freeze the lead read 10 percent of each lane's capabilities (sorted by name, every n-th). The findings below come from that read. The sample lists are not kept: they went stale with the first contract change and the completeness rules above are what the test enforces.

## Findings from reading the sample

Nothing blocks the freeze. The sample read cleanly: summaries match the declared effects, outward capabilities carry gates that match their risk (send, post, pay, delete, push_protected), and error lists are non-empty with permission codes on guarded calls. Points for the owning lane to confirm before its first build packet, none of which change a count or the hash:

- L7a writes with level `view` and no permission (`devices.revoke`, `spaces.leave`, `stars.toggle`, `identity.totp_begin`, `identity.passkey_remove`) act on the caller's own objects. Confirm the resource binding resolves to the caller, so the grant check cannot be satisfied by another person's object.
- `access.requests.decline` has no permission and level `view`. Confirm the handler requires the request's approver, since the registry alone does not.
- `governance.tombstones.reapply` is described as a system call and has no permission. Confirm its caller restriction or keep it out of the HTTP and MCP surfaces.
- `runs.undo` has risk `high` and no gate. Undo replays inverse writes, so confirm it never reverses an outward effect (the preview capability `runs.preview_undo` says what it cannot undo).
- 39 capabilities carry a gate while their effects are `write`, for example `mail.domain.remove` and `network.server_remove` (gate `delete`). This is intended: the gate applies to destructive inward writes too.

## 13 Nov 2026: a guest's grant on a thread or a channel is capped by one table (lane f-guest)

No capability contract changes and no snapshot moves. What changes is what a grant means for a guest. A guest is a person with no standing in the Space: no View on it through any role, group or grant of theirs (a role cloned from Guest, or Guest beside a harmless custom role, is still a guest; a role that carries permissions gives standing), never an agent. On a thread or a channel, kernel/src/authorize/guest-caps.ts lists what each level may do, and authorize refuses every other capability whatever the grant says, however the guest came by the grant (the channel invite, a thread shared with them, an agent's chat, a type-wide grant). A guest holds a thread or a channel only through a live grant: a guest whose access is removed or has run out reaches nothing there, reads included, owner of a reply thread they opened or not. A view guest reads, follows and marks read, and leaves the channel; a reply guest also posts, reacts, opens a reply thread under a message and edits or deletes their own messages (the handlers still refuse anyone else's). Engine settings, stopping turns, helpers, handoffs, crew, placement, terminal, modes, pins, renames, adding people, inviting and archiving are refused. A capability that is not in the table is refused: kernel/tests/authorize.guest-caps.test.ts fails when a thread or channel capability is added and not classified. Guests invited on earlier releases are held to the table at once, with no migration, because the table is read when a call is checked. A channel's own label does not cover its chat (members of a labelled channel keep reading it, as on rc.26). A grant held by anyone who has standing behaves as before.

## 13 Nov 2026: one clock for next fire times; a time in a daylight saving gap fires when the gap ends (lane b-clock, P1 step 2)

No capability contract, version, field or error changed, and no snapshot moved. The times that `scheduler.next_times`, `scheduler.schedule`, an Automation's schedule trigger, a goal's check-in and a repeating task's next date come out as are now worked out by one file, `lib/clock.ts`, instead of three cron parsers and six zone readers. Where the old code answered differently, this is the dated behaviour change, by the lead's decision of 13 Nov 2026 (P1 contract, section 8, Q2):

- A local time that does not exist on the spring-forward day (02:30 in New York on 8 March 2026) used to be skipped that day in Automations and in `scheduler.*`. It now fires at the first moment after the gap (03:00, 07:00 UTC). The same rule holds for a goal's check-in time and the end of its quiet hours. The two tests that pinned the old rule, `modules/automations/schedule/schedule.test.ts` ("daylight saving") and `modules/scheduler-api/tests/schedule.cron.test.ts` ("daylight saving"), changed to the new rule; nothing else in them changed.
- A local time that happens twice on the fall-back day fires once, at its first pass, in every zone. It already did in New York; in London and Sydney the old code fired at the second pass.
- A repeating task keeps its wall clock in the holder's profile time zone: 09:00 stays 09:00 across a change. It used to add fixed 24 hour days and drift by an hour.
- Found while recording the golden times (`lib/clock.golden.json`, 229 cases recorded before the clock existed), not named by the contract: the start of a day in a zone (`startOfDayIn`, used for Needs you and due reminders) was an hour wrong on the two days a year the clocks change, for a moment after the change. It now reads midnight. Home's Routines today asked for a routine's times in the person's zone although the routine's cron is in UTC, so a person outside UTC saw shifted times; it now asks in UTC.
- A routine's cron keeps the reading it always had, so a routine saved on a live Space does not change meaning or stop on update (review of 13 Nov 2026; `modules/agents/tests/cron.old-new.test.ts` compares the new reader with the old one, kept verbatim, over 20,000 generated expressions): numbers only, one year ahead, a 7 in the weekday field is Sunday wherever it stands ("0-7" is Sunday only, "7/2" is Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, "7-0" is accepted, "1-7" is refused), "1/3" counts up to 6, and a "*" inside a list ("1,*") is every value but not "any day". The clock itself, which Automations and `scheduler.*` use, reads these the other way (7 is Sunday only as itself, a range may end in 7, a "*" in a list makes the field "any day"); those two parsers already did.
- A sub-hourly cron that names every minute of an hour (`*/30 * * * *`) has no fire in the repeated hour on the fall-back day (New York, 1 November 2026: 05:00Z, 05:30Z, then 07:00Z): the second pass of 01:00 to 01:59 does not fire, by the rule "a time that happens twice fires once, at its first pass". Elapsed-time rhythm belongs to `{ every: { hours } }`, which counts in elapsed time. In London and Sydney the old code skipped the first pass instead.
- A zone read from a profile setting is free text: a name Intl does not know reads as UTC and never stops a repeating task from coming back.
- `lib/presence.ts` (working hours) and `modules/memory/learn-quota.ts` (the day a learning quota resets) now read the clock too; their answers did not change.
- Every-minute and every-N-minute crons now cost 0.19 ms and 0.05 ms per next-time call (measured on box B, New York, 300 and 1000 calls); the first version cost 8.9 ms and 3.5 ms.

- Compatibility: additive outputs; `files.folder.purge` and `files.trash.empty` refuse or skip agent folders.
